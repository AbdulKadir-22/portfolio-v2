# Dominated on the field. Targeted off it. 

It's been nearly a month since my last blog.

A lot has happened.  
Most of it good.  
Some of it unfair.  
All of it worth writing about.

* * *

## The hackathon that felt like destiny

I entered a national-level hackathon with what I genuinely believe was the strongest team in my college. Round one was a breeze we always knew we’d clear it. No drama, just execution.

Then came the drama.

After clearing Round 1, our fourth member backed out. A university-level competition pulled her away. We tried to convince her we were going national, this was bigger, but she had made up her mind. So we found a replacement and moved on.

When the problem statement dropped for the final round, we got to work. I designed interfaces for two applications. We built something that felt real.

* * *

## Indore, and what a hackathon should actually be

We traveled to Indore for the national round. Another team from our college came along. The journey itself was electric laughing, messing around, zero stress.

That reminded me of something important: hackathons aren't always about the tech. Sometimes they’re about the people you’re building with and the memories you make along the way.

* * *

## The project that turned heads

Inside the hackathon, we were dominating.

Our project was a multi-agent system for emergency vehicle coordination, real-time ambulance routing using a custom algorithm validated against a research paper, compared against Dijkstra's and others. We built two applications (for ambulances and traffic authorities), three websites (an admin panel, a caller interface, and an algorithm visualizer), and a full presentation suite — PPT, video, GIFs, a research report.

We presented like our lives depended on it. Because in that moment, it felt like they did.

The team wasn’t ordinary either. One of us had a research internship from Cambridge. **I had a development internship from IIT Madras.** One had submitted to a Scopus-indexed journal. And one was documenting and marketing the entire thing on Instagram in real time.

Teams were literally coming to study our project. We noticed it early the stalking, the attention. We were the ones to beat.

* * *

## Dominated on the field. Targeted off it.

We were in the top 5.

Then the finalist list changed.

We were out.

When we sat and listened to the presentations of the teams that made it through, the gap in quality was obvious and not in their favour. The difference between our work and theirs was too large to ignore. It didn't take long to understand what had happened.

We lost. Not because we weren't good enough. Because someone decided we were too good to be convenient.

That one still stings. But I know what we built. I know what we were.

> *Dominated on the field. Targeted off it.*

* * *

## The ride back, and a conversation about GSoC

We headed home.

On the way back, I had one of the most valuable conversations I’ve had in a long time. One of my teammates is a GSoC contributor. I asked him everything. how the selection works, what good proposals look like, how to pick the right organization.

I'm applying that in the very near future. That conversation alone was worth the trip.

* * *

## The side story: helping a friend ship his final year project

In between all of this, a friend needed help deploying an [ASP.NET](http://ASP.NET) application. While helping him, I ended up building and deploying my own college project — the one due in fifth semester right now, a year in advance.

I had to learn Docker on the fly. No prior understanding, full AI assistance, a Dockerfile built from scratch, deployed to GitHub. It worked.

His project shipped. His final year was complete. As a second-year student who made that happen, I was more proud than he was.

I also convinced him to join me for the next hackathon. New team forming.

* * *

## The laptop that betrayed me (and the hackathon we got into anyway)

Back home, I’d already promised a friend we’d register for another hackathon together. We did. We built. And in the final minute of submission, my laptop crashed.

We submitted anyway.

On April 2nd, we got the selection email. We were in.

At that moment I realized something quietly I have leveled up. Farther from where I started than I sometimes give myself credit for.

* * *

## What comes next

The vacation is here.

I've decided: Spring Boot and PostgreSQL are next. Cybersecurity progression continues. There's a lot of ground to cover and I’m looking forward to covering it.

This month gave me losses that felt like wins and wins that felt inevitable. Both kinds are useful.

I'm not running from anything anymore. I'm just building.

* * *

> *"You don't always get the trophy. But you always get the lesson. And sometimes the lesson is worth more."*
