# A Month of Silence, and the Work That Spoke Back

---

Exactly one month has passed since my last blog — the one where I wrote about disappearing.

I did disappear.  
 For a week. Maybe two.  
 And not a single soul tried to find me.

Strangely, I wasn’t disappointed.

Somewhere deep down, I already knew my worth in front of them — and perhaps more importantly, I learned not to measure my worth by their absence.

Silence has a way of clarifying things.

---

### Choosing Solitude Over Noise

After that disappearance, I made a quiet decision:  
 to stop chasing team-driven startups for a while and return to **personal work** — work that didn’t need validation, meetings, or consensus.

It took time.  
 Not because the work was hard — but because I needed to unlearn distraction.

And in that silence, I built two projects.

Not unicorns.  
 Not investor-ready pitches.  
 Just honest tools — made to solve problems I was living with.

Sometimes, helping yourself is the most sincere form of building.

---

### Be;Coming — Writing to Stay Alive

The first app I built is called **Be;coming**.

At its core, it’s a journal.  
Nothing fancy. No pretentious UI.  
Just a clean, notepad-like interface with dates and streaks — a place to show up every day.

But the depth lives beneath the surface.

I integrated **Gemini APIs** to rewrite my journal entries with better grammar, generate summaries, and create a personal timeline — not to erase my voice, but to help me understand myself more clearly.

Then came the decision that felt most like *me*:  
I didn’t want my words trapped in one place.

So I built:

* an API service to save entries into my **Notion database**, because I had history there and didn’t want to abandon it
    
* and another service to store entries in **Google Docs**, so that at the end of the year I can print them, bind them, and hold a year of my life in my hands.
    

Be;coming isn’t a product.  
It’s a companion.

A place where I don’t perform — I process.

---

### Streacly — Discipline, Measured Honestly

The second app is **Streacly**.

Unlike Be;coming, this one isn’t just for me and that’s why I decided to keep it **open source**.

Streacly helps people:

* create projects,
    
* track time spent honestly,
    
* filter progress by hours, days, or focus periods,
    
* and keep control without guilt or gamification pressure.
    

It’s not about streaks for the internet.  
 It’s about accountability for yourself.

Open source felt right because discipline grows faster when shared.

Github Link: [https://github.com/AbdulKadir-22/Streacly](https://github.com/AbdulKadir-22/Streacly)

### The Internship Reality Check

Back in December, I also joined an internship.

And I won’t pretend it’s been smooth.

Not because I lack skills but because I lack **time mastery**.

I spend around **3–4 hours a day** on the internship product, and because of that, **Civveo has slowed down**.

For a while, I seriously considered leaving the internship.

So I did something rare: I asked for guidance.

I spoke to my **HOD**, and his response surprised me — not with authority, but with empathy.

He told me:

* if I can secure a few proper clients for Civveo, I can focus on it,
    
* he’d handle the attendance issues for me,
    
* but I would lose attendance marks — a trade-off, not a punishment.
    

Then he said something that stayed with me:

> An internship is a few months of effort, not a lifelong battle.  
> You can slow down Civveo it doesn’t mean you’re abandoning it.

That reframed everything.

Sometimes, wisdom is knowing when not to rush.

---

### Fetchitt — Moving With Precision

Last week, another opportunity came unexpectedly.

A friend asked me to join his startup as **CTO**.  
The product is called **Fetchitt** — a peer-to-peer delivery platform.

I said yes. But this time, I set a rule for myself:

> Only take what I can execute the same day.

No procrastination. No inflated promises.

Every task I assign myself gets completed — or it doesn’t get assigned at all.

That discipline feels different.  
Quieter. Sharper.

---

### The One Thing That Still Hurts

There is one thing I’m sad about.

I haven’t been able to contribute to the **open-source organization** I had chosen for long-term contribution.

Not because I don’t care.  
Not because I’ve quit.

But because time, once again, demands honesty.

I haven’t forgotten that goal.  
I’ve only postponed it consciously.

And that matters.

### Looking Back at the Month

This month didn’t give me applause.  
It didn’t give me reassurance from others.

But it gave me something rarer:

* proof that I can build alone,
    
* proof that silence doesn’t break me,
    
* proof that progress doesn’t always look loud.
    

I didn’t disappear to be missed.  
I disappeared to see what remained when everything else stopped.

And what remained…  
was work.

---

### Closing

I don’t know if this is the perfect path.  
I don’t know if I’m moving fast enough.

But I know this:

I’m no longer running to escape my thoughts.  
I’m building through them.

And for now that’s enough. 

Ending with a thought of my own   
“If i don’t show up, I Disappear”
